# Five-minute client walkthrough
## 0:00 — The problem
“Your team can keep Excel. This concept turns an agreed export into a consistent follow-up queue, helping consultants see what to do next.”
## 0:45 — Show the daily queue
Run the app. Point out the synthetic-data disclosure. Open High-priority leads. Explain one row using its consultation, interaction and recency fields. Explain that points are business rules, not AI probabilities. Download the CSV and open it in Excel.
## 2:00 — Explain the workflow
“One person exports a workbook each morning. We validate the data, produce the queue, and the team records follow-up and outcomes in Excel. The pilot will test whether this saves preparation time and improves follow-up coverage.”
## 2:45 — Show the ML lab
“The synthetic ML example captured 70 of 172 simulated conversions in the top 256 enquiries, compared with 37 for newest-first. These numbers reflect artificial data, not a forecast for your company. Real ML would require reliable historical outcomes and activity timestamps.”
## 3:30 — Explain the pilot
Propose one team, one workbook, shadow-mode validation and a supervised trial. Keep first contact prompt and minimum follow-up for all leads. Explain the 30-day wait for final conversion outcomes.
## 4:30 — Close
“The next step is to review your current Excel structure using an anonymised example and agree on a small pilot. We can begin with rules and build the evidence for ML over time.”

## Questions to prepare for
- Cost? This prototype uses no paid AI API. Quote build/support after reviewing scope; hosting and production security may add cost.
- Will it approve visas? No; it helps commercial follow-up.
- Can we use it immediately? Validate the workbook fields and rules first, then test in an approved private environment.
- Why not ML immediately? Small or incomplete history may not support trustworthy training. Rules provide an inspectable starting point.
