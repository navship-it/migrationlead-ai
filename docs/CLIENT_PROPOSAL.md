### Proposal: help Immatrix follow up consistently while keeping Excel
**Prepared by Navya Ravindran. Independent concept; company approval and operational details remain to be confirmed.**

#### Why it could help
When a small team uses Excel, the immediate opportunity is to make follow-up ownership and priority visible. This project provides a daily worklist with clear reasons, flags slow initial responses and shows enquiry volumes by source. These are capabilities to validate with staff, not confirmed company problems.

#### A lean first scope
One existing workbook, one team and one daily CSV export. Agree transparent rules with the owner; score each enquiry at 48 hours; keep consultant judgement and a minimum follow-up standard for every lead. A small company does not need a new CRM or an ML subscription to start. First-contact handling still happens immediately through the existing process; scoring does not justify waiting 48 hours.

#### What the demo shows
A sample of fictional enquiries becomes a ranked queue. The consultant sees the rule, priority and next action, then downloads the queue to use alongside Excel. The separate ML lab demonstrates what historical data may enable later. It does not predict from customer data or claim production readiness.

#### Four-week pilot proposal
- Week 1: map the workbook, confirm fields, lead ownership, outcome definitions and the data-access arrangement.
- Week 2: clean an authorised export, agree rules, run in shadow mode and validate every output against Excel.
- Weeks 3–4: test rule-assisted worklists on a limited group; log follow-up activity, overrides and data problems.
- After the final lead's 30-day window matures: review paid-client conversion and effort. Extend observation if the sample is too small; do not declare success from a handful of cases.

Deliverables: agreed data template, daily priority export, training session, operating instructions and pilot review. CRM migration, automated messaging, document processing and production ML are outside this initial scope.

#### Define success before the pilot
Track follow-up coverage within an agreed time window, median time to follow-up, staff time spent preparing worklists, paid-client conversion within 30 days and consultant adoption. Select numerical targets after measuring the baseline. Random assignment to existing versus assisted workflow is preferable where volume and staffing permit; otherwise report before/after results as observational with seasonality and lead-mix caveats.

#### Illustrative value calculation, not a forecast
If 100 monthly enquiries convert at 10%, that is 10 clients. If a properly measured pilot later shows 12%, that is 2 additional clients. At an assumed AED 1,000 contribution per client after variable costs, the illustrative contribution difference is AED 2,000 before project and operating costs. Replace every assumption with company figures. No such improvement has been demonstrated.

#### What Immatrix would provide
An owner for the pilot, an authorised pseudonymised export, agreed definitions and brief weekly feedback. A simple activity log should capture lead ID, activity timestamp, action and outcome. Final status alone cannot reconstruct past snapshots for later ML training.

#### When ML becomes appropriate
Assess enough mature, reliable historical outcomes, representative conversions and non-conversions, timestamped features and a stable process. Start with a learning-curve and holdout assessment rather than a fixed row-count promise. Promote a model only if it improves relevant baselines on unseen real data and works in a supervised pilot.
